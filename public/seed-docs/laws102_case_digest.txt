LAWS102 — Contract Law Case Digest: Offer, Acceptance & Breach

REQUIREMENTS FOR A VALID CONTRACT
1. Consensus (offer + acceptance, genuine meeting of minds)
2. Capacity (both parties must be legally able to contract)
3. Formalities (some contracts must be in writing, e.g. sale of land)
4. Legality (the purpose of the contract must be lawful)
5. Possibility of performance
6. Certainty of terms

OFFER vs INVITATION TO TREAT
An offer is a definite proposal capable of being accepted as-is. Goods
displayed in a shop window, price lists, and advertisements are
generally treated as an "invitation to treat" — the customer makes the
offer at the till, which the shop can accept or decline.

ACCEPTANCE
Must be a clear, unqualified "yes" to the exact terms offered. Adding
new conditions is legally a counter-offer, not an acceptance — it kills
the original offer.

VOID vs VOIDABLE CONTRACTS
- Void: the contract never had legal effect at all (e.g. an illegal
  purpose, or one party lacked capacity entirely).
- Voidable: the contract is valid but one party may choose to cancel it
  (e.g. contracts entered under misrepresentation or duress) — until
  cancelled, it remains binding.

MISREPRESENTATION
A false statement of fact that induces the other party to contract.
Categorised as:
- Innocent — made honestly believing it to be true
- Negligent — made without reasonable grounds for believing it true
- Fraudulent — made knowingly false, or recklessly, to deceive

REMEDIES FOR BREACH OF CONTRACT
- Specific performance — court orders the breaching party to actually
  perform their obligation.
- Cancellation — the innocent party may cancel and be released from
  further obligations (only for material/serious breaches).
- Damages — monetary compensation to put the innocent party in the
  position they would have been in had the contract been performed.

REVISION TIP
When answering a problem question, always work through the six
requirements above in order before discussing remedies — most marks are
lost by jumping straight to "breach" without first confirming a valid
contract existed.
