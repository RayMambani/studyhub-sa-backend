ACCT101 — Financial Statements Quick-Reference Summary

THE ACCOUNTING EQUATION
Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity
Every transaction keeps this equation in balance — that's the whole
logic behind double-entry bookkeeping.

INCOME STATEMENT (Statement of Profit or Loss)
Shows performance over a period (e.g. "for the year ended...").

    Revenue
    - Cost of Sales
    = Gross Profit
    - Operating Expenses (rent, salaries, utilities, depreciation)
    = Net Profit (or Net Loss)

BALANCE SHEET (Statement of Financial Position)
Shows financial position at a single point in time ("as at...").

    Non-Current Assets   (property, equipment, long-term investments)
  + Current Assets        (inventory, receivables, cash)
  = Total Assets

    Non-Current Liabilities (long-term loans)
  + Current Liabilities     (payables, short-term loans)
  = Total Liabilities

    Total Assets - Total Liabilities = Owner's Equity

CASH FLOW STATEMENT
Splits cash movement into three categories:
- Operating activities  — day-to-day trading cash flow
- Investing activities  — buying/selling long-term assets
- Financing activities  — loans, owner contributions, drawings

KEY ADJUSTING ENTRIES TO REMEMBER
- Accrued expenses: recognise the expense even if not yet paid.
- Prepaid expenses: only recognise the portion "used up" this period.
- Depreciation: spread an asset's cost over its useful life.
- Bad debts: write off receivables unlikely to be collected.

QUICK RATIOS WORTH KNOWING
- Current ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
- Gross profit margin = Gross Profit / Revenue x 100
